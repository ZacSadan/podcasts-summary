Hackers breach the CIA's OSINT platform, cyber security firm Radware gets hacked, the US investigates Iran over 50 ship hacks, and threat actors exploit an Azure API bug to access cloud environments. This is the Risky Bulletin, prepared by Catalan Kimpanu and read by me, Claire Aird. Today is the 25th of November, and this podcast episode is brought to you by NTAI. In today's top story, hackers have breached an OSINT system at the US Central Intelligence Agency. According to the New York Times, the breach exposed unclassified data. The incident also impacted the office of the Director of National Intelligence. In other news, a threat actor hit a web skimmer script on a subscription payment widget used by 80 US newspapers. Affected news sites include The Boston Herald, The Denver Post, The New York Daily News, and The Sun Sentinel. The script was active for nine days from November 13. It was planted in the Navigate payment widget. The script stole data via a web RTC connection rather than normal HTTP requests. Hackers have scraped customer data from an internal API belonging to cyber security firm Radware that was accidentally exposed to the internet. The stolen data includes customer contact details and some credentials used for cloud log storage. The company has notified clients and advised them to rotate compromised credentials. A threat actor has added malware to six plugins from WordPress plugin developer WP Connect. The plugins deployed a backdoor to WordPress sites for several hours on Sunday. The WordPress team permanently removed the plugins after detecting the attack, but site owners will still need to manually remove the backdoors. The US state of Colorado took its mobile app offline on Monday following a security breach. Officials say the app will remain offline while they investigate the incident. The app is used by residents to store digital state driver's licenses and to file documents with state services. A hacker stole credit card data from payments processor Cleverbridge on November 4. The company is notifying affected customers and urging them to check statements for fraudulent transactions. B2B software provided Glotopper completely rebuilt its cloud environment last month following a security breach. The company says hackers accessed application code, databases and API and integration keys for customer environments. Known for its digital gift card distribution, Glotopper expects to lose $2.5 million in sales due to the hack. Hackers have stolen the personal data of more than 8,000 clients from the online share trading division of Korean conglomerate Samsung. The breach affected the online broker's customer support system and was detected on Saturday. Stolen data includes customer names, mailing addresses and ID scans. An Elasticsearch database left misconfigured on the internet leaked more than 1.6 billion records from what appears to be a Chinese identity verification system. The data includes names, national ID numbers and phone numbers as well as school enrolment and business registration details. Security firm UpGuard says the data covers more than 840 million Chinese citizens and appears to have come from an underground commercial data lookup service. Hackers have stolen $13.5 million in tokens from the Wagyu cryptocurrency platform. Wagyu's founder said the hackers breached its web hosting provider, 1984 Hosting, and stole a developer key from old logs. 1984 Hosting was hacked last week and shut down its entire platform in response, bringing down more than 20,000 websites. 1984 Hosting is Iceland's largest web hosting provider. Cryptocurrency exchange ChainFlip suspended operations over the weekend after hackers stole $12 million worth of assets. According to blockchain security firms, the attackers appear to have gained access to one of the platform's bolt keys. The stolen funds were almost immediately laundered through the Tornado cash network. ChainFlip's own token lost a third of its value following the hack. The US Department of Justice has filed a lawsuit to recover $1.1 billion worth of crypto assets stolen from Binance last month. The DOJ wants to seize almost $2.9 billion of the $6 billion stolen by North Korean hackers. More than $1.2 billion has already been laundered and is considered unrecoverable. The remaining crypto was frozen by Tether and Circle. The US government is investigating Iran over a series of hacks that hit almost 50 ships carrying oil, LNG and other chemicals. Some of the breaches were first made public on Iranian and Russian Telegram channels. Until now, only a handful of the ship hacks had been publicly reported. The White House has nominated Pentagon Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies to serve as National Cyber Director. Davies was named Acting Director on Friday after the Trump administration fired the former director, Sean Cancross. Director of National Intelligence, Jay Clayton will serve as the Acting Director while Kirsten Davies goes through the Senate confirmation process. The government of Kazakhstan is turning its I2 instant messaging app into a full-blown government platform. The app will provide access to about 75% of government services by the end of the year. Officials say I2 will be modelled after China's WeChat and Russia's Max Apps. Lapsas Hacking Group member Arion Kurtage has been released from prison on licence. In 2022, he hacked and attempted to extort Rockstar Games, Revolut and Uber. He was initially deemed unfit to stand trial due to severe autism and was remanded in custody at a secure hospital in December 2023. He was sent back to prison in January this year after doctors found him fit to stand trial. A judge sentenced Kurtage to seven years in prison but released him on a serious crimes licence the same day due to his time served. Kurtage's release conditions include a ban from using Telegram and Discord. Security firm Checkpoint says recent law enforcement takedowns and major hacking forums have fragmented the underground data-breaking industry. The company says the number of breaches and data leaks has increased but threat actors are posting less on major forums. According to the company's research, data brokers have been pushed to trading in smaller, more private channels. Canadian authorities have arrested 75 suspects who were contracted via social media to commit various crimes. The suspects range from 12th to 24 years old. They also filmed themselves to prove they'd carried out their tasks. The criminal activity ranged from arson to physical beatings and more. Cybersecurity firm Checkpoint has discovered a previously unknown Iranian APT group that primarily spies on Cyprus. The Volunt Manticore Group is targeting the Cypriot telecommunications companies, airlines and critical infrastructure. Checkpoint believes the group operates under Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security. A Russian APT group has launched a new Signal and Telegram phishing campaign. The campaign initially targeted Ukraine but has since expanded to target lawmakers, journalists and civil society figures across Europe. The group tricks targets into linking their accounts to attacker-controlled devices. The campaign has been linked to a Russian APT group, Tractor's Frontier Blizzard, Sentry Bear and UNC 5792. Hackers have exploited a vulnerability in the Azure API Management Service to access corporate cloud environments. Norway's Cybersecurity Agency says the bug was exploited to breach two energy companies, Equinor and Skagarak Energy. Microsoft patched the bug on November 19 and notified customers to rotate secrets stored in the API Management Service. The vulnerability affected the classic version of the service, the V2 version was unaffected. Hackers are exploiting a vulnerability in a legacy anti-CSRF library to take over ASP.NET servers. The library was developed in the early 2000s to protect ASP.NET apps against cross-site request forgery attacks. The project has been retired since December 2008 but is still present in many older apps and websites. The vulnerability allows attackers to run malicious code service that accept malicious user-supplied serialized data. And finally, Russian tech giant Yandex has updated the Yandex browser license agreement to remove any mentions of user privacy and anonymity. The new terms allow the company to collect personal data, IP addresses and browsing history. The new agreement came into effect in October. And that is all for this podcast edition.
