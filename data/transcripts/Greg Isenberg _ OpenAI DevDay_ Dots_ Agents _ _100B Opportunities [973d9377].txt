Numbered Section Summaries

1. The Launches Worth Your Attention

OpenAI shipped 20-plus features at Dev Day 2026, and I focus on four: Dots, the Decisions API, the Agents API, and Sign in with ChatGPT. I cover GPT 6.1 Soul, Ultrafast, Private Intelligence, and Spaces briefly at the end.

2. Dots and the Plugin Comeback

Dots is OpenAI's answer to Muse by Meta and Instinct. A user asks for help, like fixing a permit or finding a restaurant, ChatGPT understands the job, and it selects a plugin, which is essentially a mini app. By my paraphrase, Sam admitted plugins got mixed reviews before, and now he plans to make them a thing. I think billions of dollars of transactions will flow through personal agent platforms, and maybe trillions over 10 to 20 years.

3. Decisions API, OpenAI's Answer to Jev

The Decisions API is in limited preview. It focuses Luna's intelligence on user-defined questions with finite, predefined answers, so developers can classify content, route requests, or choose an agent's next action. I'm hooked on Jev, and I see UI design as an underserved use case, like finding the highest-converting app store screenshots and direct response websites.

4. Agents API and Vertical Harnesses

The Agents API now supports computer use and brings Codex's multi-agent capabilities, tool search, tool calling, and context compaction into your application. OpenAI runs the infrastructure. If you have great data, a great brand, or deep knowledge of a vertical, I would be playing with this today.

5. Sign In With ChatGPT and Free Core Apps

Users can now sign in with their ChatGPT login and use their token allowance on other websites. I compare it to Facebook login, which helped create billion-dollar companies like Yelp. People hesitate to pay $20 or $30 a month for another AI app, so I would make the core app free and charge for enterprise features, team sharing, and more data. Before, the builder paid for inference, which made a free core app hard.

6. Sell Workflows Over Tokens

Tiny AI categories are now economically viable, like an agent that cleans architectural CAD files, a researcher that monitors one scientific field, a Shopify catalog cleanup desktop app, a video tool built around one editing workflow, or a contract review tool for one type of franchise agreement. The value comes from proprietary information, a specialist workflow, access to outside systems, collaboration and auditability, a network of humans or suppliers, and a measurable business result.

7. Trigger, Decision, Action, Feedback

My framework starts with a trigger, like an invoice coming due, inventory running low, or tax time. ChatGPT decides what to do next, an application takes the action, and feedback shows whether it was approved or rejected. The strongest businesses will own the trigger, the action, and the feedback.
