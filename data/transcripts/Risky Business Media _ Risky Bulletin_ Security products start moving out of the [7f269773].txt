Security products start moving out of the windows kernel, the FBI gets hacked again, the US Department of Agriculture also gets popped and Austrian, Bulgarian and Portuguese governments get owned too. This is the Risky Bulletin, prepared by Catalan Kim Panu and read by me, Claire Aird. Today is the 23rd of November and this podcast episode is brought to you by NTAI. In today's top story, eight cyber security vendors are releasing updated products that will rely far less on Windows kernel drivers. The updated EDR and antivirus software moves some functions out of the kernel and into the new Windows endpoint security platform, a collection of user mode APIs. Microsoft began development of the platform in 2024 after a faulty CrowdStrike kernel driver update crashed eight and a half million Windows computers. CrowdStrike is among the vendors making changes alongside ESET, Sophos, Bitdefender and Trendmicro. In other news, hackers have breached the FBI's National Crime Information Centre. The NCIC manages a national database of active criminal investigations. It holds warrant and fugitive information and is used for background checks. This is the Bureau's second breach this year after the Shiny Hunters Group breached the agency's HR portal in September. The US Department of Agriculture has taken several applications offline after its security team discovered a breach. According to NextGov, the apps were used by the agency to issue payments to American farmers. The apps have been down for more than a week, reportedly due to the agency resetting all of its database passwords. Hackers have stolen employee data from Austria's foreign ministry. Investigators believe the hackers entered through a third-party provider running its official website. Officials have not yet attributed the attack. The Bulgarian government has evicted a cyber espionage group from its networks. The unnamed group had been inside the state administration's networks for at least five years and had stolen tens of millions of government documents. Officials said the intruders managed to evade detection because the government's networks are fragmented and lack a single integrated monitoring platform. They also said there was no conclusive evidence to link the hackers to a specific government. Portugal's Transportation Agency has temporarily suspended all services following multiple cyber attacks. The agency confirmed the attacks on Friday and says there's no evidence of data theft. It's halted services to protect its data. The IMT is the fourth Portuguese government body to be targeted by cyber attacks in recent weeks. A threat act has exploited a vulnerability in Microsoft's Entraconnect Health API to scrape tenant data. The attacker collected administrator emails and general tenant configuration data. Microsoft says the bug was introduced in a July update. The company deployed a fix on Thursday and has notified affected customers. Hackers have breached Rackspace spot marketplace accounts to steal cloud resources. Rackspace says the hackers used authentic credentials that were leaked by a third party to access the accounts. The intruders used their access to steal Kubernetes secrets from customer accounts before deploying their own pods. A threat act have breached the router maker GLINET and deployed malware on customer routers and KVM devices. The attacker exploited a vulnerability in the company's cloud remote management services. GLINET released firmware updates on Sunday that removes the malicious code from customer hardware. A cyber attack has disrupted a Nissan IT platform used by mechanics to diagnose mechanical issues in the company's cars. The platform has been down since November 13. The outage was initially believed to be a technical issue but has since been upgraded to a cybersecurity investigation. Without the platform, service centres can't diagnose cars, update firmware or replace lost key fobs. Fundraising platform DonaPerfect has taken its systems offline following a cyber attack last week. The company has not said if customer or donor data was stolen. At the time of recording, DonaPerfect had started bringing systems back online. Online gambling giant, FDJ United, has taken down its websites following a cyber attack on Friday. The attack impacted sports betting platform Unibet and several online casinos. The company didn't provide further details about the attack. A cyber attack has disrupted Iceland's largest web hosting provider. 1984 hosting shut down services on Friday to protect customer data. Tens of thousands of customer websites are currently offline. The company says the attacker used AI to breach its systems. UK cybersecurity officials and private sector researchers have taken thousands of industrial fridges offline across the UK to protect them from hackers. The secret campaign took place last month after hackers destroyed millions of euros worth of product at French supermarkets. Security researchers discovered that fridges made by British firm Resource Data Management were similarly insecure. They worked with UK cybersecurity agencies to take the units offline and later with the vendor to roll out a patch. The Pentagon has instructed employees to remove common access cards from card readers when they're not actively using them. The Pentagon says the NSA has detected malware that's capable of abusing card stored credentials to authenticate to government systems. The White House fired National Cyber Director Sean Cancross on Friday. Pentagon Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies was appointed to take over his duties until a replacement is chosen. President Trump reportedly approved of the firing after Cancross clashed with other officials over AI security. The Senate confirmed Cancross in August 2025. A member of the No Name 057 pro-Kremlin hacktivist group has been arrested in Spain. The suspect created the group's first physical cell in Spain and was recruiting other members. He was also involved in online channels where he called for cyber attacks against the government and violence against police. Authorities discovered a copious amount of weapons and ammunition at his home. Russian authorities have charged three suspects with large-scale trafficking of personal data. The data included copies of passports, tax identification numbers, credit reports, and income and real estate information. One of the suspects ran the scheme while serving a sentence in a correctional colony. The other two suspects carried messages out of prison and executed his orders. The group made almost $50,000 from the scheme. The US Department of Justice has charged the operator of a Florida-based ransomware recovery firm with wire fraud. One Ricardo Palacio Escobar operated the Ransomware Help.com service, which he claimed reverse-engineered ransomware strains to decrypt victims' files. The DOJ says that, in reality, Escobar was paying ransomware groups, adding his cut on top and decrypting files with attacker-supplied keys. New Delhi police have arrested a 14-year-old boy for hacking and selling government data. The teen is accused of breaching a military recruitment and training program. Officials say the suspect was trying to sell the data of 4,000 UGNV users on the dark web for $500. The US Federal Communications Commission has kicked eight companies out of US telecommunications networks for robocalling. The eight providers had failed to respond to requests for information about suspected illegal robocalls. The agency has removed more than 1,400 companies from US telco networks since January 2025 for failing to follow robocall mitigation rules. A massive malvertising operation is targeting iOS users with old exploit kits. According to Confiant, the Moldy-Ruse campaign buys programmatic ads at scale and redirects users to more than 480 domains hosting the exploit kits and has been active for at least six months. Moldy-Ruse used Corona, Darksword, Silkpath and Ghostwave for exploits kits that are capable of exploiting unpatched iPhones. The campaign delivered malware to steal crypto wallet seed phrases, Keychain data, messages and photos. An attacker is compromising GitHub reposts, hijacking their actions and using them to scan for known vulnerabilities in Node.js servers and exploit them. The scans are targeted React2 shell and a Node.js authorization bypass. According to the open source malware team, the campaign has scanned over 20,000 servers and successfully compromised nearly 300 of them. Security researchers have linked a new version of the Badbox ad fraud botnet to Chinese electronics manufacturer, TXCZ. The company's domains and GitHub repositories are hosting the botnet's command and control infrastructure and deploying malicious payloads. Human Security says it found the malware on TV set-top boxes where it loaded and ran video ads in the background. The botnet also operated as a residential proxy network. This is the third Badbox version that researchers have disrupted over the past two years. Google will start enforcing Android Developer Verification from January next year. The program requires app developers to prove their identity to Google. Only apps from verified developers will be allowed on the official Play Store. The verification program was announced in 2025 and has been under testing in four countries since September. And finally, Signal will update its Android and iOS apps to block AI assistance from taking screenshots to read private conversations. Signal's rolling out the new feature after both Apple and Google announced new AI capabilities for their mobile operating systems.
