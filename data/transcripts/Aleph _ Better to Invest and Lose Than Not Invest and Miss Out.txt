[SHOW NOTES]
Most people think the biggest risk in venture capital is making a bad investment. Oren Zeev thinks that's completely wrong — and it's the reason he manages $3 billion alone.

His argument: if you make a bad investment, you lose 1x your money. If you miss a company that could have been 100x, you didn't just lose — you failed to make 100x. Those are not the same thing. Not even close.
This asymmetry is why Oren left traditional partnerships and went solo. In a large firm, partners rarely push each other toward a deal. The dynamic almost always runs the other way: "Have you looked at this risk? Have you checked that?" Smart, well-meaning people slowing you down, asking the right questions, protecting the downside. And most of the time, that's fine.
But it only takes one time. One deal where a partner's skepticism slows you down just enough to lose it — or kills your conviction just before you were going to pull the trigger on something truly great. One missed 100x. And suddenly none of the five times they saved you from a bad investment matters.
The best investments in any fund are the top one or two. Everything else is noise. Oren built his entire operating model around protecting his ability to make those calls — fast, alone, and without having to convince anyone.
