An open SSH authentication bypass bug hits Alpine Linux and open WRT, hackers attempt to raise fridge temperatures at French bloodbanks. A French bank gets hacked for $133 million, and the Thai opposition has been targeted with spyware. This is the Risky Bulletin, prepared by Catalan Kimpano and read by me, Claire Erd. Today is the 6th of November, and this podcast episode is brought to you by Sublime Security. In today's top story, the Open SSH project has released a security update to patch a bug that lets attackers authenticate with any random password, as long as it exceeds 256 characters. The so-called SSH whole vulnerability only works against open SSH versions running on Linux distros that use the Muscle C library as standard. This includes Alpine Linux, Open WRT, Buildroot and Yocto. The bug impacts all open SSH versions released in the last decade. In other news, hackers unsuccessfully attempted to raise the temperature of bloodbank refrigerators across France this week. Staff noticed the sudden temperature rise and intervened. The French Blood Establishment Agency says no plasma bags were compromised in the attack. Hackers have stolen $133 million from French banking group Khredi Mutul Akaya. Blockchain security researcher Zach XBT discovered the theft this week, as the stolen money was being laundered on the blockchain. The bank is working to recover at least some of the funds. Digital devices connected to the White House computer network are running residential proxy apps. According to journalist Brian Krebs, at least one of the residential proxy apps is owned by a Russian company that was linked to cyberattacks against the 2016 US election. Hackers have breached the internal network of Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The intruders stole the data of 3,600 employees, including their names and email addresses. The hack took place at the end of July. The Ministry says the secure part of its internal network was not impacted. Hackers have stolen the personal data of more than 20,000 grant applicants from Norway's International Aid Agency. NORAD, the Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation, says the hackers breached its network via 27 compromised accounts. The agency is now working with the country's National Security Authority to investigate the intrusion. NORAD works under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It distributes international aid to foreign organisations that promote human rights, climate protection and sustainable development. A student hacked into a system used by schools in Iceland in an attempt to alter grades and attendance data. According to local media, the student exploited a login vulnerability in the inner system which is used by at least 16 upper secondary schools in Iceland. Japanese gaming company Sega has temporarily suspended its arcade game network following a cyber attack this week. The hack impacted the Sega All.net and Amy Networks. The company is investigating whether user data was stolen. Supermarket chain Ahol del Hayes expects to lose up to 250 million euros in sales due to a cyber attack that defrosted its freezers last month. This projected figure is separate to the millions of euros worth of products that were spoiled as a direct result of the attacks. Hackers hit Ahol's supermarkets in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Romania. Hackers have stolen customer data from blockchain hosting platform Chainstack. The incident took place last month. Chainstack says the attacker used AI tools to speed up and automate the intrusion. A threat actor redirected 15 cryptocurrency-related domains to wallet-draining phishing sites in late October. The incident was traced back to a compromised customer support account at the domain registrar named.com. Affected crypto platforms include Flap, Kintsu, Pickle Finance and Origami Finance. Apple has notified two Thai opposition party figures that their devices were targeted with mercenary spyware. The people's party leader, Natapong Ruonpanyawut and the Democrat party leader, Abhisit Wachachiwa, disclosed the attacks in a press conference this week. The two demanded the government confirm its involvement. The Thai government is a known NSO group customer. Ukraine's intelligence service has dismissed two high-ranking officials as part of an anti-corruption investigation. The two allegedly accepted bribes to allow local scam call centres to operate. The arrests are part of a broader crackdown against local scam centres that led to the firing of Ukraine's prosecuted general in September. Two Canadian men have been charged with deploying cryptocurrency miners on hacked US university systems. The campaign ran for three years from 2016 and even breached a US supercomputer. That breach allegedly caused damages of $337,000. One of the suspects was extradited and arraigned in a New York court this week. Cryptocurrency companies have quietly pulled the pin on an industry group established to counter crypto crime groups. The T3 Financial Crime Unit was established in 2024 and was backed by Binance, Tron, Tether and TRM Labs. According to Bloomberg, the group helped freeze more than $250 million in stolen assets last year, but seized all activity this August without explanation. A Chinese cyber-espionage group has carried out a DNS hijack against the Sogo Input Method software, delivering malicious updates to Chinese speakers abroad. The malicious update installed the dark vigil back door on targeted systems. ESET says the hackers behind the operation have been active since last year. The company tracks them as slippy nunchucks. Russian espionage group APT29 is conducting a large-scale watering-hole attack against Android and iOS smartphones. The group is using hacked websites to run malicious scripts on the devices to steal cookies, Wi-Fi passwords and other data. The campaign is targeting Ukraine and users' older end-day exploits. Thread actors are exploiting a recently-patched race-conditioned vulnerability in WordPress. According to Pycos Security, the bug allows an attacker to replace a WordPress site's configuration file during the update process. The bug was patched last week, and Sysa added it to the Kev database this week. Syksal has released security updates to patch an actively-exploited zero-day in its GS1900 Switch series. The vulnerability has been exploited since June. Attackers used it to deploy the jitterly back door and six-sut root kit on almost 1,000 devices. According to security firm Binerly, an earlier patch didn't actually fix the zero-day. The exploitation of the Syksal devices was part of a larger campaign linked to the Red Heron Group that also targeted other technologies. SonicWall will not release fixes for two vulnerabilities in its firewall management platform. The two bugs allow attackers to read files and execute SQL injection attacks against the company's so-called global management system. SonicWall retired the platform in June 2025. Bitdefender researchers have crafted an app installation package that can put Android devices in a crash loop. The technique is possible due to how Android passes the data stored inside an app's zip installation package. The malformed data can make Android OS map the app to a memory space it didn't allocate and trigger an infinite crash loop. The crash loop also triggers after a phone restart, even if a device is in safe mode. And finally, the US Department of Justice has fined two defense companies a combined $13 million for lying about their cybersecurity posture to obtain government contracts. ILCDover and ILC Astrospace stored controlled unclassified information at a cloud provider that lacked FedRAMP accreditation. The two companies also failed to implement cybersecurity controls on their networks and patch vulnerabilities. They had contracts with NASA, the US Army, and the Missile Defense Agency.
