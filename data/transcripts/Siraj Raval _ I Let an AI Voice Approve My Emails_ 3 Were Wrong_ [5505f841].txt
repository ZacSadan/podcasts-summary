My AI agent already does real work. It reads my inbox, drafts my replies, flags the ones that need a human and moves things on my calendar. It has been doing it for months and it talks like a log file, so I skim it, and skimming isn't checking.

So I added one layer: a voice, built on Fish Audio S2.1 Pro. One function, text in and audio out, English and Hindi in the same voice. Then I ran an experiment I didn't enjoy. One working session, approvals by voice only, log window closed. At the end I opened the log on camera and counted every decision I'd approved without reading: how many were correct and how many were wrong.

I said I'd publish the number before I knew what it was. It's 24 correct, 3 wrong.

The rule I came out with, which is yours to steal: give your agent a voice for output, never for approval. Voice is a great narrator and a terrible auditor. That's about what a confident delivery does to a listener, not about the speech model, which read every wrong sentence perfectly.
