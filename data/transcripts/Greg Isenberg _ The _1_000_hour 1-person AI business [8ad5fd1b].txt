Numbered Section Summaries

1. Why James Sells to Main Street 

James started out owning agencies for local businesses and startups, running performance ads, social, and SEO. He owns Diesel Dudes, a mobile diesel repair business in Charlotte that dispatches mechanics to broken-down semi trucks, and he grew it with ads, funnels, and local SEO. He packages that experience into how he'd start an agency again today.

2. The Market and the Niches 

Roughly 112,000 plumbing and HVAC companies average about $2.5 million a year, only 14% of owners say AI is fully part of how they run, and they pay around $129 per lead. James steers toward urgent, high-ticket work like commercial hood cleaning, dental implants, tree services, irrigation, sport courts, artificial turf, chimney sweeping, gutter guards, septic, crawl space, and backup generators. I'd add snow removal in the Northeast, since the trades around where you live are your unfair advantage.

3. Funnels as the Offer 

Agencies still charge $20,000 for a good funnel, and anyone who knows a coding agent can build one fast at high quality. For an HVAC company, James tests a free check, a $79 tune-up tripwire, an instant price, a repair-or-replace quiz, an owner video sales letter, and an advertorial, each tied to its own ad campaign. A tripwire liquidates ad spend: at $1 a click, 20 visitors cost $20, one $20 sale covers it, and the rest leave an email address.

4. Pricing, Proof, and Mindset 

A plumber with 100 leads a month at $129 spends about $13,000, and one $1,714 job pays for 13 of those leads. James suggests an intro install plus monthly testing and reporting, possibly performance pricing later, and a first free build in exchange for a case study. I believe you have to commit to being the best person to help plumbers in Dallas for six to 18 months, and James recommends one market and one geography for six months.
