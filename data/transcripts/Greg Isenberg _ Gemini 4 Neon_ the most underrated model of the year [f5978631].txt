Numbered Section Summaries

1. Community Builds That Show the Range 

Chetasula shared Neon's motion design for a Pelican product, and as someone who builds apps and hunts for mascots and motion design, I find it stunning. Another builder's piece got me thinking about hardware companies and prototypes, which ties back to my Argon episode on vibe manufacturing. Neon feels strong at this kind of hardware work.

2. Physics That Holds Up 

One builder pushed Neon to add uneven limestone terrain to stress test robot dog biomechanics, and it added per-paw slope adaptation, five-point obstacle scanning, and spine torsion. It reminds me of the Boston Dynamics dogs, and I can picture whole games and worlds built this way. Next to GPT 6.2 Astra on the pelican-riding-a-bike test, Neon's physics feel like a different world.

3. Games and Apps From a Prompt 

One builder made two pinball games with Blender and WebGPU, with generated play field art, PBR and stained glass maps, multiball physics, and procedural audio. They remind me of the Windows XP pinball game, and they're live with people playing them. 

4. Five Ways to Get the Most Out of Neon 

I set thinking to high, and I build inside Antigravity instead of the bare wrapper, which explains why people on X split so hard on this model. I use the orchestrator for daily work and /swarm for big builds, assigning a researcher, a front-end designer, a front-end engineer, and a back-end engineer before I go to sleep. I always give it a verification loop (open the browser, click buttons, fix bugs), and I put context first and demand facts, which triggers automatic caching and stops drift.

5. Lead Magnets and Trojan Horse Demos 

Neon builds playable lead magnets, 3D configurators, and mini games that capture emails and get shared on X and Instagram, work agencies charge $10,000 to $100,000 for. For a Trojan horse demo, I paste in a business URL, like a Chick-fil-A in Toronto, let Neon scrape it, build that store a calculator or tool, and send it over. If I were 20 and chasing cash flow, I'd redesign stale websites and offer them for $50 or $100 a month.

6. Finance, Back Office, and Overnight Products 

Neon is great with data: messy invoices go in, and a working financial model and audit come out. I ran my own company's numbers through it, and it feels like having a CFO-like partner. It also handles lead routing, CRM ops, and reconciliation for less than the competition, and with swarms, agent teams research, build, and QA micro products and directories while I ship.
