Key Points

• Instinct hides the agent complexity behind a phone number and iMessage, so a first-time user starts in seconds.
• Remy gets it to book a haircut, hold a restaurant table, file a Bali visa on arrival, and open an Emirates Skywards account.
• A spend-limited virtual card keeps the blast radius small when the agent pays for things.
• The agent stalls when a task needs a phone app or an Indonesian checkout page.
• Users report that Instinct keeps copies of email after they disconnect Google, so treat privacy as an open risk.
• The trusted person network lets one Instinct talk to another, which builds network effects into the agentic era.

Numbered Section Summaries

1. The Invite-Only Launch

Remy first sees Instinct on X, where large AI accounts talk about it. The site shows a wait list, so he grabs an invite code from a public tweet. Scarcity drives the curiosity, and Remy reads the rollout as a marketing play as much as a compute limit.
    
2. Onboarding Built for the Mass Market

Remy signs in with a phone number and lands straight in iMessage. The agent opens with photo examples: flight bookings, check-ins, smoothie orders, subscription cancellations. Remy teaches founders AI for a living, so he values a flow that skips MCPs, CLIs, and markdown files.
    
3. The Workspace, the Connectors, and the Vault

A simple web workspace sits beside the chat. It holds contact methods, connectors for email, Slack and Granola, and a vault for logins, cards, and addresses. Remy connects his email accounts and stays impressed by how much context Instinct pulls about him.
    
4. Real Tasks: Haircut, Restaurant, Visa

Remy asks for salons in Copenhagen. Instinct researches spots, pulls reviews, sends photos, emails the salon, and confirms the booking hours later. It then holds a table at Bistro Central, files the Bali visa on arrival and the arrival card, and opens an Emirates Skywards account with a saved password.
    
5. Human Touches: Reactions, Memory, and Game Pigeon

Instinct reacts to messages, reads a thumbs-up as approval, and uses loud effects and confetti. It recalls the monitor from an earlier chat and adds it to the visa arrival card. It even plays a round of Game Pigeon Cup Pong, which surprises us both.
    
6. Network Effects, Privacy, and the Ten-Billion Question

The founder Noah announces a trusted person network, so one Instinct can talk to another. I compare the invite rollout to Clubhouse in 2020, which raised at $4vbillion. Meanwhile Claire Vo and Peter Yang report that Instinct keeps copies of their email after they disconnect Google, and the terms raise the same flag.
