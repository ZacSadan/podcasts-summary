Today is the 20th of November and this podcast episode is brought to you by THINXED, the makers of the much-loved THINXED Canary. In today's top story, Microsoft will ship a new feature that will allow apps to check the security health of a Windows system before they run. The feature is called Windows baseline protection and it can check for the presence of secure boot, signed drivers and EDR software, among other things. Depending on their policies, apps can use the results of a baseline check to refuse to run, disable some features or to prompt users to enable a security feature. Baseline protection is out in preview and will be generally available to all users next year. In other news, the cyber attack has taken down the European Commission's internal HR platform, SISPA. EU officials have been unable to access the platform for almost a week. SISPA is used to manage time sheets and salaries, training registration and leave requests. The attackers use the RTEX pen testing framework to stage the attack, which took place in late October. Hackers have compromised at least 10,000 accounts on Germany's e-government platform, BundID. The accounts were compromised in a brute force attack last weekend. Officials have locked the compromised accounts and are notifying affected individuals. Hackers breach cybersecurity and IT provider Enables, cloud platform on Monday. The attackers accessed mail-assure and spam expert service, but Enables says it found no evidence that customer details were stolen. The company is rebuilding the affected services. Hackers have stolen sensitive data from almost 50 Irish credit unions after breaching a shared IT provider. According to Ireland's public broadcaster, RTE, the attack targeted a company named Wellington IT. Stolen data may include saved payee details including names, account details and addresses. Several impacted credit unions have notified the Irish government and are investigating the hacks. Hackers have stolen the data of more than 15 million customers from a test environment at Rackerton's Securities Division. More than 4.4 million of the compromised accounts contained sensitive information. No passwords, pins or other authentication data was stolen. Canonical will ship AI-generated security patches in Ubuntu Edge releases. All fixes will be reviewed by a human before they're shipped. The company cited the increased number of AI-discovered bugs and the need to provide temporary fixes as a reason. The US government has launched a new initiative aimed at taking down scam and sextortion operations. The Threat Disruption Centre will operate under the US Homeland Security Task Force. It will see US law enforcement agencies work with private sector partners to speed up the identification and disruption of scam networks. US authorities have arrested a Russian national who illegally surveilled a US tech company. Igor Du entered the US illegally through Mexico and received orders from his Russian handlers via Telegram. He purchased surveillance equipment to spy on the company's employees near their workplaces and homes. Du was instructed to spy on the target because it was supplying weapons to Ukraine. The targeted company is believed to be Palantir. A US judge has sentenced a Scottish man to six years in prison for hacking and crypto theft. Tyler Robert Buchanan was a member of the Scattered Spider hacking group when he hacked a dozen US companies including telcos, tech firms and cloud providers between 2021 and 2023. He and his accomplices then used their access to steal more than $11.6 million from cryptocurrency owners via sim swapping. He is the fifth Scattered Spider member to be sentenced to prison. Ukraine cyber police arrested a man who sent bomb threats to thousands of schools in the Czech Republic, Slovakia and Latvia last year. He used Telegram and email accounts to send the threats. The suspect was initially identified in July last year but fled to Belarus to avoid arrest. He was arrested in September after returning to Ukraine. Dutch police have arrested multiple suspects who were using stolen data to plan violent thefts across Amsterdam and The Hague. The suspects were using data stolen from Revolut in September to target high earning individuals. Hackers posed as law enforcement officials to obtain the personal data of some Revolut customers. Officials said the suspects are French and Belgian nationals. Ukrainian authorities have arrested four members of a ransomware and data extortion gang. Officials say the group made more than $40 million from attacks on 28 companies alone. The group allegedly has 27 members including former members of the Conti ransomware gang. A hacker has compromised a Microsoft Python library that's used to build AI agents. The incident took place on Tuesday and impacted the Microsoft agent framework PyPI package. The malicious payload was an info stealer that stole cloud and CICD credentials. A new cyber crime group calling itself the audit team has been hacking and extorting companies in multiple countries under the guise of security audits. The group uses the ops agent framework to breach targets then it demands payment to release the results of their so-called audit. The group then leaks sensitive data stolen from the companies if they don't pay. The group's been active since January this year and has breached dozens of firms. Instant messaging service Telegram is working on a new feature that links user accounts to SIM cards. The new system is being developed to comply with Indian legislation. The Indian government gave Telegram and other messaging services until the end of the year to comply with the new rules and roll out the feature. Broadcom has released a security update for the VMware vCenter server that's being exploited in the wild by a Chinese APT group. The Zero Day allowed the group to bypass authentication and access the server's directory service. Google linked the attacks to a group it tracks as Armory Castle. The same group was also behind a Zero Day in Beyond Trust remote access applications last month. The Apache Software Foundation has released a security update for the Apache Roller blogging platform to fix an actively exploited Zero Day. The vulnerability can allow attackers to run malicious code on blogs in a classic untrusted deserialization attack. The Apache Roller team says an attacker took a bug it patched in September and modified it to work on older versions of the software. The Zero Day now impacts all roller versions released over the past two decades. A collection of five vulnerabilities can allow attackers to gain root access on EngineX web servers. Named EngineX2 root, the vulnerabilities target the server's HTTP2 implementation. EngineX2 root works on most servers and only requires that HTTP2 over TLS is enabled. A proof of concept is publicly available on GitHub. EngineX is the world's most widely used open source web server running on more than 225 million websites. According to Census, there are more than 9.4 million EngineX servers that run vulnerable versions. The Ruby programming language has fixed a memory corruption bug that can let attackers run remote code on Ruby apps. Named Ruby2Shell, the vulnerability impacts many Ruby apps and frameworks in their default configurations. Ruby on Rails, Sinatra, GitHub Enterprise and GitLab are all exploitable. Security firm Depth First has disclosed a new remote code execution bug in the PHP programming language. Named PHP Aftershell, the vulnerability can be used against servers that use PHP file-based session storage. The PHP project released patches for PHP Aftershell and 16 other vulnerabilities on Thursday. Drupal has released a security update to patch a remote code execution vulnerability. The bug allows threat actors to abuse the Drupal installer to reinstall the site and run their own code. The bug is not universal and is only exploitable in certain scenarios. And finally, security firm Sophos has released a security update for the Sophos Unified Threat Management Platform that fixes a pre-authentication remote code execution vulnerability. According to security firm Intruder, the vulnerability allows attackers to gain root on the devices by sending an email to organizations that use the appliances.
