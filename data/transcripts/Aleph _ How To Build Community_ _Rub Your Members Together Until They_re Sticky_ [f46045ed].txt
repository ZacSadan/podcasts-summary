[SHOW NOTES]
On this episode of Invested, Michael Eisenberg sits down with Douglas Atkin, former Global Head of Community at Airbnb and author of The Culting of Brands, for a conversation about why people join cults, what that reveals about brands and politics, and how you build something people genuinely belong to. Douglas explains what he found when he set out to study the most extreme forms of belonging — that cult members are almost always ordinary, well-educated, socially capable people, and that they join, stay, and leave for exactly the same reasons as members of brand communities.
Michael and Douglas trace that insight from a focus group of teenagers talking about sneakers, to Meetup, to Airbnb — where Douglas spent three and a half weeks interviewing hundreds of hosts and guests and came back with the purpose that still runs the company: create a world where anyone can belong anywhere. He tells the story of what it cost to live that purpose, from taking the Attorney General of New York to court over a subpoena for 15,000 hosts' data, to giving two non-marketers veto power over a marketing hire, to Airbnb turning away over a million would-be customers who wouldn't sign its core values.
They also go deep on the darker mechanics of belonging: how demonization defines a group and manufactures solidarity, why the far right has run that playbook so effectively, what the decline of religion has to do with the rise of cultic politics, and what Douglas would actually prescribe for the loneliness epidemic.
They discuss:
- Why the people who join cults are completely normal — and why that matters
- The four Ds of difference, and why a cult is a new idea while a sect is a splinter
- How demonization creates belonging by defining the in-group and inventing a threat
- Why brands took over religion's role, and why politics is now taking over from brands
- The first golden rule of community: rub your members together until they get sticky
- Why it takes showing up four times before a community becomes hard to pull apart
- How Airbnb found "Belong Anywhere" by talking to hundreds of hosts and guests
- Why a purpose is only real if it costs you something in the short term
- The New York subpoena fight, and organizing hosts into political activists
- Core values interviews with veto power, and turning away a million customers
- The community commitment curve, and how to move people from clicking to face-to-face
- Why working from home was a bad idea, and what actually treats loneliness
If you want to understand community, cults, purpose, culture, movement-making, and why belonging is both the best and the most dangerous thing about human beings — this episode is essential listening.
