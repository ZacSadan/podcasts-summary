[SHOW NOTES]
Yuval Ariav argues universities can't teach critical thinking while showing students only one set of views.

Asked what happened at Columbia, Yuval calls it a "neglect of academic duty." His argument: you can't teach people how to think without repeatedly putting competing views in front of them. The bias, he says, often isn't a professor preaching from the front of the room — it's quieter, in which readings get assigned and which narrow slice of recent history a course chooses to react to. Michael, who teaches at the business school, weighs in. "Don't think you can teach people how to think without repeatedly showing them competing views."
