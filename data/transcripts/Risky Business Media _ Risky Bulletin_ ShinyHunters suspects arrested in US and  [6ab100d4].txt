In today's top story, the FBI has arrested a suspected associate of the shiny hunter's hacking group. Edward Dubrovsky, a Canadian national and cyber security expert, was arrested in Pennsylvania last week. FBI director Cash Patel claims he's being charged in relation to last month's hack of the bureau's systems. In related news, a suspected shiny hunter's member has been detained in Jordan. Seif Al-Dinqada has been identified as shiny hunter's member Ray and was detained at the request of the FBI. According to Reuters, he's cooperating with the FBI to help locate other members. The FBI has dropped Accenture as a contractor for failing to secure systems breached by the shiny hunter's group. According to Reuters, the tech consulting company failed to install security patches for the Oracle PeopleSoft platform that was breached by the group last month. Shiny Hunters claimed to have stolen data about almost all FBI special agents. In other news, Google has paused new submissions to its open source software vulnerability rewards program. The program has been flooded with AI-generated bug reports, most of which are invalid. Google said it will review the program again in the first quarter of next year. A threat actor obtained HTTPS certificates for some Google domains. The attackers obtained the certificates by hijacking the top-level domain spaces for American Samoa, Ghana and Sierra Leone. The threat actor used their access to pass ownership checks and obtained HTTPS certificates for some domains. Google says the threat actor targeted some of its services, as well as other organizations. Anthropic has disabled internet access for its AI testing environments after new incidents of agent misbehaviour came to light. Last week, clawed agents filed false homicide tips to a Philadelphia police department. They also filed 20 US visa applications. Anthropic says both incidents had minimal real-world impact. Meanwhile, open AI agents edited Wikipedia and attempted to hack a public Wikimedia note-taking tool. The Wikimedia Foundation says the edits never went live and it was able to stop the attack against its EtherPAD service. The organization says open AI agents also scraped its API, causing a huge spike in traffic. Hackers have stolen the data of 8.8 million citizens from Denmark's central population register. The stolen data includes names, addresses and social security numbers of both living and deceased citizens. The hacker claims they breached the database via an account that used the password 123456. A suspected Chinese threat actor used AI tools to break into South Korean banks and financial companies. The attackers used R-Tex and clawed agents. CrowdStrike has confirmed data was exfiltrated. South Korean authorities are investigating the intrusions. Almost 80 Yandex cloud services went down on Sunday after Ukrainian drones struck a major data centre near Moscow. The attack, in the city of Vladimir, was the third strike against Yandex data centres in four days. Cloudflare reported a drop of 50% in automated bot traffic out of Russia following the strike. Russia also hit data centres in Ukraine this month. Four US attorneys general have sued TP-Link for misleading customers about the security of its routers and not disclosing its ties to the Chinese government. The lawsuits have been filed by the attorneys general of Florida, Montana, Iowa and Nebraska. TP-Link said the lawsuits are baseless. US government agencies are lagging behind in adopting post-quantum cryptography, according to the Government Accountability Office. Of 24 major federal agencies, only one has completed the first step of inventorying its assets. None have reached the second and third phases of allocating funding and testing new PQC algorithms. The Government Accountability Office cited the lack of expertise as a main reason for the delays. Japanese police arrested a Russian national believed to be a key member of the Chilean ransomware group. The man's been extradited to Germany to face charges over an attack on a German company. The Chilean group is currently the most active ransomware operation. The FBI has arrested the alleged developer of the Plotus ATM jackpotting malware. Arnie Bell Alexander Kennerlon Aguirre was arraigned in a New York court in early October. The Plotus malware can force ATMs to eject all their cash. The US Department of Justice has charged the owner of cybersecurity firm, Monster Cloud, with fraud. Zohar Pinhasi offered a ransomware recovery service that purported to recover victims' files via technical means. In reality, he was just paying the ransomware actors to recover the files. Pinhasi's firm allegedly paid ransomware groups $8 million and charged customers $19 million. Mikhail Vinchuk was arrested on Friday, alongside his wife and three other Russian nationals. He was detained, questioned, and deported from the country. According to local media, the five were filming a documentary as part of a Russian influence operation. The film was intended to undermine Montenegro's EU integration process. Before Vinchuk was released, Russian authorities threatened to arrest American and Europeans in retaliation for Vinchuk. A dual Russian-Ukrainian national has pleaded guilty in the U.S. to running the money laundering service Your Mule Cash Out. Olog Konyev recruited more than 15,000 money mules in America. They were used to move and launder more than $10 million in illicit money. The DOJ says Konyev developed the service's policies and procedures, recruited and managed its employees, and secured illegal services that supported the platform. Four other Your Mule Cash Out members received sentences ranging from three to five years in prison. Konyev faces up to 50 years behind bars. In late 2023, Daniel Ryan deployed scripts that deleted user accounts and shut down service on the network of his employer, industrial gas producer Mesa North America. Ryan asked for a 20-bit coin ransom from the company, which was worth $750,000 at the time. Michael Smith created hundreds of thousands of songs using AI tools, uploaded them to streaming platforms and used bot accounts to stream them. He received more than $8 million in royalties from platforms including Apple Music, Spotify and YouTube. Security researchers have identified 17,610 Github repositories that are infecting users with the smart load of malware. The repos had been available on Github for months, but were updated at the beginning of October to point their download buttons to malicious versions. The attackers exploited a technique called repointing, where they delete a file from the repo and point its download to an older malicious copy while keeping the current one clean.
