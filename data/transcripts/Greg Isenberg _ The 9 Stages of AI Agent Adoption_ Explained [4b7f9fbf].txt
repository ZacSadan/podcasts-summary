Numbered Section Summaries

1. Who Blocks and Who Welcomes 

Amazon started blocking Meta's Muse agent around September, Delta treats outside booking agents as unauthorized automated activity, and Yelp lets agents in only through its paid data licensing program. In the middle, Zillow welcomes agents acting for real customers while its anti-scraping tools sometimes stop them, and Walmart, a Muse partner, called its Muse failures an accident. On the welcoming side, Meta works with Walmart and Stripe on an open standard, Shopify lets merchants sell through AI channels, and Visa and Mastercard build ways to recognize trusted agents.

2. A Customer Who Reads Instead of Looks 

I think of an agent as a customer with a credit card who reads instead of looks. It skips your $100,000 homepage video, banners, and countdown pop-ups, and it reads your product details, prices, shipping costs, and return policy. When I ask my agent to find a replacement air purifier filter, compare delivered prices, and check with me before buying anything over $50, I give it a goal, rules, and a limit.

3. The Ladder of Trust 

Agents climb three rungs: research, prepare, and pay. Most companies accept the first rung because it works like search, and the whole war happens on the third, so I see businesses making a mistake when they block all three. An agent compares five suppliers in the time it takes me to open one tab and stays loyal to whatever worked last time, so a stuck checkout quietly sends it to a competitor.

4. Why Companies Block 

Some reasons to block hold up, since an agent might place 50 orders instead of five, the customer calls the store about it, and an airline has to prove you authorized a booking. Money drives a lot of it, because Amazon earns tens of billions a year from ads and airlines profit from seat upgrades, bags, and credit card offers that agents skip. Amazon and Delta also build their own agents

5. The 9 Stages

Banks fought Mint.com until Plaid built a proper connection between apps and banks, and the banks moved from blocking to signing deals to charging for access. I see agents on that same path, from blocking today to an arms race to a split where sales businesses let agents in, data businesses charge, and giants build their own, then a Plaid moment for agent identity and payments. After that, welcoming companies win customers, everyone competes for agents with faster checkouts and pages written for agents, ads come back to buy an agent's first recommendation
