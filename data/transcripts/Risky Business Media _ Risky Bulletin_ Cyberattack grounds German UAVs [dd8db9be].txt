Germany grounds UAVs after a supply chain attack. A threat actor accessed Barracuda's cloud for two days. The US AI task force asked for regulatory leniency, and the FBI disrupts a North Korean face-swapping operation. This is The Risky Bulletin, prepared by Katelyn Kim Panu and read by me, Claire Aird. Today is the 16th of November, and this podcast episode is brought to you by THINKSED, the makers of the much-loved THINKSED canary. In today's top story, a cyber attack has targeted Quantum Systems, a German defence company that supplies drones to the Ukrainian military and planted a malicious update in the company's flight planning software. Quantum Systems says the malicious file was never delivered to any drones. The German defence force temporarily grounded drones made by Quantum while the company investigated the incident. The drones are no longer grounded. In other news, the newly established White House AI Task Force has encouraged federal agencies to show leniency toward organisations that report AI-powered breaches. The task force has requested 12 months relief from harsh penalties. Members of the task force met with private sector representatives last week to encourage companies to report AI-powered hacks to authorities. A threat actor has breached Barracuda's cloud environment. The hack took place earlier this month, and the hacker had access to the company's cloud network for almost two days. Barracuda says the threat actor used a cloud access key issued to a third-party AI provider. The attacker used the RTEX AI framework to breach Barracuda's cloud, where they searched for credentials and crypto wallet data. US authorities have disrupted a North Korean operation that stole more than $400 million from cryptocurrency developers by deep fake video calls. Authorities linked the so-called Face Stealer campaign to the Blue Norov espionage group. The FBI seized a server holding more than 900 recordings of previous victims' video chats with the group. Blue Norov operators used deep fake footage to trick targets into installing malware on their devices. An international law enforcement operation has led to the arrest of more than 350 dark web vendors. Arrests were made in 14 countries and targeted drug and weapons dealers, among others. The suspects were vendors on dark web marketplaces previously seized by authorities, including archetype Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tortudor. The city of Mobile, Alabama, paid $14.7 million to cybercriminals after city staff were tricked into changing a contractor's payment details. The scammers spent five months convincing city staff to change a contractor's bank account details. The fraud was discovered a week after a legitimate invoice was paid to the scammers, and the contractor reported the missing payment. Hackers have stolen the personal data of 22 million users of a digital mail and document management system called MaleEva. The platform is owned by LaPost, one of France's largest postal and courier groups. Most of the affected users are corporate employees who received paychecks, invoices and business invites via the system. Hackers have leaked the personal data of 46,000 employees of semiconductor giant TSMC. The hackers tried to extort the company for $200,000 in Bitcoin before posting the data on an underground hacking forum. In its SEC filing, the company said the data was taken from a cloud service provider and that its network was not compromised. Cryptocurrency wallet maker Trezor has shipped a firmware update to brick devices that contain spy chips. The company says most of the impacted owners bought their wallets between August and September this year from a reseller named Crypto Billus. Trezor has cut 80% of its supply and network following the incident. Crypto Billus has also been linked to spy devices found in ledger wallets. A ransomware attack took down Belarusian cloud provider ActiveCloud last week. The company has restored web hosting and email services, but almost a week later some services are still down. No ransomware group has taken credit for the attack. Three other Russian cloud providers were also hit with similar hacks over the past month. The non-data extortion group has leaked data from Expedia after the company refused to pay a ransom. The online travel agency confirmed the breach last week and said the hackers accessed its network via a third-party data platform. Expedia is the world's second-largest travel agency by revenue. Hackers have leaked the personal correspondence of John Mark Duggan, a former US Marine and sheriff's deputy who fled to Russia and now runs a disinformation group. The archive chats cover half a decade and detail how Duggan cooperated with the GRU and FSB to set up and operate the Storm 1516 group. The group uses forged documents and disinformation to undermine Ukraine and Western alliances. Hackers have stolen $2.23 million in crypto from the Arcus cryptocurrency exchange. The platform says the attack has exploited a flaw in its user accounting system to withdraw funds they were not entitled to. Arcus says the hack triggered its withdrawal rate limit, which blocked another $3 million from leaving its wallets. Security firm Checkpoint has released a firmware update to patch an actively exploited zero-day in its firewalls. The local privilege escalation vulnerability is being chained with a PHP2 shell exploit to gain root access on the company's devices. According to the vendor, the attacks have compromised dozens of customers. Hackers are exploiting a vulnerability in the Telegram widgets feature to run PowerShell commands on vulnerable systems. Security firm Kaspersky says attackers were using the vulnerability to steal credentials from targeted systems and install the web rat malware on them. Telegram has patched the bug for Android but not for Windows. Hackers are exploiting a vulnerability to unreal IRCD software to take over IRC servers. The attacks began last week, one day after the patch was shipped. The vulnerability allows attackers to inject and run commands on the servers. Threat Actors are exploiting two new vulnerabilities in Copeland X Web Evo refrigeration controllers. Attacks began two hours after the bugs were publicly disclosed last week. The exploited bugs are a subset of 14 vulnerabilities discovered by security firm Voncheck. The company analysed Copeland's firmware after its devices were targeted in a wave of freezer hacks throughout Europe last month. Copeland did not release patches because the X Web Evo product line reached end of support in March. The two vulnerabilities allow attackers to run OS commands or write to existing files without any authentication. Google's Elite Bug Hunting Unit will gradually reduce its vulnerability disclosure deadline to 30 days. Starting next year, Google will give companies 60 days to patch bugs before publishing details. In 2028, that deadline will be reduced again to 30 days. The company currently allows companies 90 days to patch with an additional 30 if the bug is complicated. And finally, GitHub will pause its dependabot security tool on December 8 on repositories that have been inactive for more than a year. The tool displays security alerts when projects use vulnerable dependencies. GitHub says it needs to pause the tool for old projects in order to keep the service running smoothly for active repos.
