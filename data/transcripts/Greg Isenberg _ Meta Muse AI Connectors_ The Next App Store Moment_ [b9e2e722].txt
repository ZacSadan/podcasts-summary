Numbered Section Summaries

1. The App Store Moment for AI

Apple opened the App Store in 2008, and by June 2010 it had paid developers a billion dollars from paid apps and in-app purchases. Muse follows the same model, with Meta building the assistant and outside businesses supplying the services that complete a customer's request. As of recording, Muse is the number one app in the App Store.

2. Your Service in the Middle of a Request

Someone organizing a small event starts with a venue, later realizes they need to rent equipment, and an equipment rental company becomes relevant at that step. Meta now has a submission process, a directory of approved connectors, and editors who can feature them. An app store feature once brought me about 40,000 downloads a day for free, so being early can help you learn faster than others in your category.

3. How a Muse Connector Works

In my example, I ask Muse for a two-hour podcast studio on Tuesday afternoon with a $200 budget, the connector asks the studio's API for a room, and the service books it once I approve the $160 offer. Duffel already does this in travel, where its Muse integration lets people search flights and manage bookings, and the company has passed a billion dollars in annual transaction value. If you already have software, check what customers can do through your API today, because a useful connector might sit inside your product.

4. Custom Connectors, Review, and Open Questions

Meta documents a custom connector option, so I can connect my own Muse to a service through its API and test the integration before pursuing a public listing. Stacktree's founder published a firsthand account of the review form, which offers options for an API or an existing MCP server and asks for product information, usage examples, documentation, and support details. My open questions are how many users Muse will reach and how many connectors Meta will approve, and whether review will look like YC at 0.01% or like the Apple App Store.
