Oren Zeev's investment in Navan — which netted him roughly $1 billion — started with a chance encounter on University Avenue in Palo Alto, one block from Café Venetia.
He spotted the founders walking down the street. They'd just sold their previous company, StreamOnce, to Jive, and were still finishing out their vesting. Oren invited them to lunch. They ate. In the last five minutes, they mentioned almost as an aside: "Oh, by the way — we're thinking about something new." That was it. No deck, no product, no company name.
Oren immediately said: let's talk tomorrow.
The next day, co-founder Ilan showed up with mockups he'd built overnight. By the end of that meeting, Oren had committed to fund the company. They raised a toast with prosecco. Between the two conversations, Oren had called three or four CFOs he knew and asked them one question: would you actually switch from what you're using for corporate travel? Every single one said: "I hate what I'm using. Yes."
That was enough.
From a two-minute street encounter to a term sheet in under 48 hours — this is how one of the biggest returns in recent venture history began.
