Key Points

• Keep one project per Grok Bot account, so context stays clean and tokens stay available.
• Start with a chief of staff bot, and let it audit your business and name the first three agents.
• Follow the four weeks: build the team, execute, hire and fire, then automate.
• Ask each agent for a five-line brief: what shipped, what is stuck, what needs you.
• Move repeatable steps into scripts or make com, and keep agent tokens for high value work.
• Newsletters and directories give the easiest entry, and the two models feed each other.

Numbered Section Summaries

1. One Project, One Agent Team
Billy runs each Grokbot account on a single mission. Several businesses in one account cause context bloat, slow work, and heavy token use. He pays $200 a month for a second account that holds his Shopify experiment alone.

2. Design as a Feature
Grokbot gives you a limited set of agents, and Billy counts roughly 76 to 88 shape and color combinations. One thread per bot works like a direct message to a teammate, so he finds each agent fast and saves switching costs.

3. The Chief of Staff Audit
Billy picks a chief of staff first and connects it to Notion, Slack, and Gmail. On day one it takes stock of the business and names the top three agents that drive revenue. His audit produces a research agent, a sales agent, and a Beehive platform expert.

4. Build, Execute, Expand, Automate
Week one builds the team. Week two stays on execution alone. Week three fills the real gaps, such as an inbox agent. Week four adds routines, so the team moves the ball while Billy sleeps.

5. Routines and Five-Line Briefs
Each agent sends a short daily or weekly report to the chief of staff: what shipped, what is stuck, and what needs the human. Five lines keep tokens and context tight, and the chief of staff routes the rest of the work.

6. Teach Agents to Review Like You
Billy runs adversarial QA loops with a panel of subagents across three rounds, and output moves from about 50 percent done to about 90 percent done. He then turns a feedback thread into a skill, so another agent reviews work in his own style.

7. The Newsletter Machine
A research bot checks his source list twice a week and fills Notion cards with restaurant news and events. A make.com step formats each blurb the same way every week, and Beehive sends the issue on Thursday. His sales agent watches the Gmail inbox, prices the ad slots, builds a sales sheet, and found a local bagel shop sponsor this week.

8. Directories, Shopify, and the Site Builder Stack
Billy runs a stack of Cloudflare, Porkbun, Astro, and GitHub, and he shipped a live booking page from the beach in two or three hours. For directories, a research agent writes a brand page as markdown each day and a coding agent publishes it. He calls a newsletter plus a directory the lowest barrier entry point for a Grokbot business.
