Key Points

• A point of view creates the moat, because copycats must wait for your next idea.
• Content sits in three tiers: commodity, personality, and original. Each tier adds different leverage.
• Ownership equals association. Volume builds it, and personality details make it strong.
• Your life story is the unmade data set, so AI learns it only from your own writing.
• Every piece sits on a spectrum from timely to timeless, so match your expectations to the type.
• Humans do the thinking and the writing. Robots do the repeating and the remixing.

Numbered Section Summaries

1. The Point Of View Moat

Cole answers the popular claim that AI removes the moat from SaaS. He says a moat appears when a product carries a method, such as Typeshare and its personal language model idea. A competitor who copies features must still look back at the person who does the thinking.
    
2. Language Is Open Source

Cole describes the alphabet, words, and most groupings of words as shared property. He uses the example of a health sentence that millions of people repeat. This explains why readers label some writing as AI slop: they recognize a commodity sentence.
    
3. The Three Tiers Of Content

Cole separates commodity content, personality content, and original content. Commodity content helps beginners, and it stays useful as a first step. Personality content and original content raise the long-term value of your library.
    
4. Approved Language And The Digital Brain

Cole spent a decade as a ghostwriter, where he assembled client language from books, blogs, and recorded meetings. He calls this approved language, and he sees AI as the automated version of the same job. A larger library gives you more net new combinations.
    
5. Ownership As Association

Cole defines ownership as association. Volume creates the first layer, and personality details create the second layer. I add Ramit Sedi and Dave Ramsey as two people who own opposite sides of the same commodity idea about coffee and savings.
    
6. The Unmade Data Set

Cole explains that everybody uses the same models and the same public data. Your formative experiences, mistakes, and life facts stay outside that data until you write them down. He gives the example of his World of Warcraft sentence, which he repeated in hundreds of pieces after one Quora answer went viral.
    
7. Timely Versus Timeless

Cole puts every piece on a spectrum. A meme captures 24 hours of attention, and an essay serves you for ten years. He asks one question before you start: how much value do you extract, and over what time horizon?
